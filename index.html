Mission Briefing

One trade problem. Three ways to prove it.

You and your team become a trade intelligence unit. You'll identify one focused international trade problem — a tariff shift, a supply-chain dependence, a regional trade deal, a sanctions regime — and build a full evidence base around it. Then you'll interpret the political economy behind it: who wins, who loses, and whose interests shape the outcome.

Your Digital Trade Report is where the analysis lives. The Roundtable is where you defend it against other teams and new evidence. The Data Video is where you take the strongest numbers and explain what they actually mean. You're assessed on evidence and reasoning — not on which side of the argument you take.

The 14-week journey

What you'll actually explore

Each week builds toward your investigation. By the time you sit in the roundtable, you'll have the analytical toolkit to defend any finding.

Week 01

Conceptualization of International Trade and Political Economy

"Why do countries trade — and who really decides?"

Trade isn't just buying and selling. It's about power, interests and who benefits. You'll meet the main actors and see why trade is never just economics.

Week 02

Historical Evolution and Institutions

"Who built the rules of the world economy?"

The World Bank. The IMF. GATT. The WTO. Where they came from, what they do — and why the system looks the way it does today.

Week 03

Approaches to International and Political Economy

"Free trade, or protect your own?"

Mercantilism, liberalism, structuralism, protectionism. Four ways of seeing the world economy — and how they still shape arguments today.

Week 04

Structures of International Political Economy

"What actually makes the global economy run?"

Production, finance, security, technology. Four structures that decide who gets what — and why some countries win more than others.

Week 05

Major Ideas and Debates

"Smith vs Keynes — who was right?"

Two giants, two visions. Free markets or state intervention? Financialization, casino capitalism, and why the argument never really ended.

Week 06

The International Trading System

"What keeps the system from falling apart?"

Principles, rules, institutions. Most Favoured Nation, National Treatment — the two ideas that hold the whole system together.

Week 07

Trade Policy and Instruments

"Every tool a government can use."

Tariffs, quotas, subsidies, anti-dumping, safeguards, non-tariff barriers. The full toolkit — and when each one actually gets used.

Week 08

Trade Negotiations and WTO Governance

"How do 164 countries agree on anything?"

The WTO's structure, negotiations, rules, and the dispute settlement system. Plus why global trade governance is under real pressure today.

Week 09

North–South and South–South Trade Relations

"Why is the game tilted — and who's changing it?"

Developed vs developing. Market access, bargaining power, special and differential treatment — and the rise of South–South trade.

Week 10

Regional Trade and Economic Integration

"Why do countries form clubs?"

Free trade areas, customs unions, common markets. The politics and economics behind regional trade blocs — and how they fit with the WTO.

Week 11

Trade, Development and Global Production

"Who actually makes your phone?"

Global value chains, multinational corporations, foreign direct investment. How trade drives development — and why it doesn't always.

Week 12

Trade, Security and Strategic Interdependence

"When trade becomes a weapon."

Sanctions, export controls, critical supply chains, economic coercion. Why interdependence can be both a strength and a vulnerability.

Week 13

International Trade and the Muslim World

"What connects the OIC economies?"

Trade patterns across Muslim-majority countries. Energy, commodities, intra-OIC trade — and the challenges of deeper economic integration.

Week 14

Contemporary Challenges and Future of International Trade

"Where is trade going next?"

Trade wars, digital trade, supply-chain shifts, environmental rules. The forces reshaping trade — and what the system might look like in ten years.
